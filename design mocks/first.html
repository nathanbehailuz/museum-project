ACCESSION NO. 94.4.431 · DEPT OF EUROPEAN SCULPTURE & DECORATIVE ARTS

Flower vase (Tulipiere)

Manufactured at The "Greek A" Factory (De Grieksche A), active 1658–1811

Date Created ca. 1695–1705 CE
Culture / Geography Delft, Netherlands
Medium Tin-glazed earthenware
Dimensions H. 8 1/4 in. (21 cm)
psychology_alt Botanical Iconography & Function

Designed specifically during the aftermath of Dutch "Tulipomania," this multi-spouted vessel allowed precious cut tulip stems, hyacinths, and lilies to receive independent hydration while supporting their heavy petals. Each spout isolates a bulb's flowering bloom, transforming the object itself into an artificial bouquet.

Cobalt-blue surface painting borrows Chinese Wanli porcelain motifs, intermingling mythical Chinese dog/lion spout terminals with indigenous European carnation and chrysanthemum sprays.

Provenance Chronicle

Acquired via Gift of Mary Mandeville Johnston, 1894. Recorded in 18th-century inventory of Dutch aristocratic estates.
